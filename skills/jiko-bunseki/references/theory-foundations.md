# Jiko Bunseki v2 — theory foundations

These public theories guide question intent only. The checklist uses original wording and is not a
validated or normed assessment. None of these concepts produces a score, diagnosis, role decision,
company decision, or hiring prediction.

## RIASEC

Use the six activity families as a vocabulary for what the user may want to explore: hands-on work,
investigation, creation, helping/explaining, persuasion/leadership, and organization. Record an
interest hypothesis and ask what experience could verify it. Do not display a RIASEC code or map a
response directly to an occupation, role, or company type.

## Social Cognitive Career Theory

Separate interest from the user's perceived ability to learn, expected outcomes, goals, barriers,
and supports. “I am interested in AI but unsure about mathematics” is recorded as interest plus an
uncertain self-efficacy and a perceived barrier. It is not evidence that the user cannot pursue AI.
Do not treat a perceived barrier as a confirmed skill gap without separate evidence.

## Self-Determination Theory

Use autonomy, competence, and relatedness to help the user describe conditions that may support
motivation. These are personal preferences and reflection prompts, not a company-culture score.
Translate a confirmed preference into an investigation question such as decision ownership,
training quality, feedback practice, or team respect.

Keep relatedness separate from collaboration preference: someone may want respectful connection and
still prefer focused individual work.

## Career Construction Theory

Use the user's episodes to notice repeated actions, meanings, and problems they want to solve. A
pattern such as “organizes complex information so others can use it” is a hypothesis until the user
confirms it. Narrative repetition is not proof of personality, ability, or job fit.

## Writing boundary

Do not copy commercial assessment items or present this custom reflection as SPI3, Gallup, Hogan,
or any other validated instrument. Keep raw responses, episode evidence, confidence, and unknowns
separate so the user can review the interpretation.
