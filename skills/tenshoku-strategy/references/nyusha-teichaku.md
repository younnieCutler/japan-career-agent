# 入社手続き・定着計画 (Onboarding and First-Phase Checklist)

This is a document and observation checklist, not tax, insurance, immigration, employment, or legal advice.
Forms, deadlines, eligibility, and probation terms depend on the user's situation and current official guidance.
Record the exact source and date; if a requirement is not confirmed, keep it `Unknown`.

## 1. Documents to inventory

Create two lists and mark each item `Confirmed`, `Unknown`, `Contradictory`, `Stale`, or `Low Confidence`.

**From the current employer, if applicable:**

- employment-insurance and pension/health-insurance information;
- withholding-tax or income documents;
- separation or employment certificates requested by the user or new employer;
- company property, access, and handover records.

**For the new employer, if requested:**

- identity, tax, pension, insurance, dependent, payroll, and bank information;
- qualification, graduation, authorization, or reference-check documents;
- the written labor conditions and any probation document.

Do not assume that a document is mandatory, that an employer will obtain it automatically, or that a missing
document always creates a tax consequence. Ask the employer or the relevant official authority and record the
answer. Money-related external claims belong in `_shared/career_claims.yml`.

## 2. Tax, insurance, and authorization questions

Use the user's actual dates and status:

- last working day and first working day;
- any gap between employments;
- residence/visa status and work authorization;
- dependents and municipality where relevant;
- which employer or authority requested each form.

The assistant may prepare a question list. It must not calculate an obligation or make a legal conclusion from a
generic scenario.

## 3. Reference checks

Treat a reference check as an employer-specific process. Confirm:

- whether the employer asked for consent and what information will be requested;
- who may be contacted and whether the current employer is excluded by the user's instruction;
- the role, dates, scope, and achievements that the candidate can substantiate;
- what the candidate should do if the requested process conflicts with a privacy or safety constraint.

Do not coach a referee to exaggerate. Preserve the candidate's request and the employer's response as evidence.

## 4. Probation and early onboarding

Compare the actual role, location, schedule, compensation, and evaluation expectations with the written offer.
Record any difference with dates and ask the manager/HR for clarification. A probation label alone is not a
prediction about the user's future; applicable terms must come from the documents and current official guidance.

## 5. Role-specific plan

Use a 30/60/90-style plan only as a user-editable planning template, not as a universal standard:

| Phase | Questions to confirm | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Initial phase | Who decides, what is the first deliverable, and how is success described? | manager message / role document |
| Next phase | Which deliverable is in scope and who accepts it? | ticket, document, or review note |
| Review phase | What was completed, what remains unknown, and what support is needed? | dated review and user's reflection |

If the written conditions and the lived role diverge, preserve both records and explain the trade-off. Do not tell
the user to stay or leave automatically.
