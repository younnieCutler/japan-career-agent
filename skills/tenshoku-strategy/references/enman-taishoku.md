# 円満退職 (Graceful Resignation) Guide

> A successful job change is decided not only by the new beginning but also by a clean parting with the existing organization.
> In Japan this is called 円満退職, and it is taken very seriously for business reputation management.

---

## Legal Framework

| Employment type | Legal minimum notice | Basis |
|-----------------|----------------------|-------|
| 正社員 (permanent, no fixed term) | 2 weeks (Civil Code Art. 627 §1) | Resignation takes effect automatically 2 weeks after notice |
| 契約社員 (fixed-term) | In principle until contract end | But immediate termination is possible with やむを得ない事由 (Art. 628) |
| 派遣社員 (dispatch) | Per the dispatch contract | Notify both the dispatch agency and the client |
| SES contract | Per project | Consult the SES company's sales rep first |

**Practical standard:** Legally it's 2 weeks, but in practice notice **1–3 months ahead** is the Japanese
business manner. If the work rules (就業規則) specify "1 month" or "3 months," following that is the key to 円満退職.

## Boss Meeting Protocol

### Preparation
- Convey the intent to resign **first** to your **direct manager (直属の上司)**
- Telling HR or colleagues before your manager destroys the trust relationship
- Avoid busy times; book in advance: "ご相談したいことがあるのですが、お時間いただけますでしょうか"
- Secure a **one-on-one space** such as a meeting room

### Meeting script

**Opening:**
> 「お忙しいところお時間をいただきありがとうございます。突然のご報告となり恐縮ですが、退職を決意いたしました。」

(Thank you for your time despite being busy. I'm sorry for the sudden report, but I have decided to resign.)

**Resignation reason (safe phrasing):**
> 「かねてからやりたいと考えていた○○の分野に挑戦したいという思いが強くなり、転職を決意いたしました。」

(My desire to take on the ○○ field I had long wanted to pursue grew strong, so I decided to change jobs.)

**Expressing thanks:**
> 「○年間、大変お世話になりました。ここで学んだ経験は、今後のキャリアの大きな財産です。」

(I've been deeply indebted for ○ years. What I learned here is a great asset for my future career.)

### Key Rules
- Take the form of a **'報告 (report)', not '相談 (consultation)'** — show firm resolve, but politely
- **Never** mention dissatisfaction with the company
- You don't have to reveal the new company's name (if asked, you can deflect with "まだ正式には決まっていません")

## Counter-Offer Response

When you state your intent to resign, the company may try to retain you with a raise or promotion.

### Why declining is wise

- The moment you state your intent, the trust relationship inside the org has already changed
- Statistically, a large share of employees who accept a counter-offer leave again **within a year**
- A counter-offer is often just **buying time** until a replacement is hired
- It sets a precedent for colleagues that "stating intent to resign gets you a raise," causing org-management problems

### Decline script

> 「大変ありがたいお申し出をいただき、心より感謝しております。しかしながら、今回の決断は十分に考え抜いた上でのものです。これ以上ご迷惑をおかけしないためにも、予定通り退職させていただければ幸いです。」

(I'm truly grateful for the very kind offer. However, this decision was made after careful thought. To avoid
causing further trouble, I'd appreciate being allowed to resign as planned.)

### Exception: cases where acceptance can be considered
- The dissatisfaction is **a single reason (e.g., salary)** and it is **concretely resolved**
- The new job is **not yet finalized**
- Even then, always consider the shift in your standing within the org due to "conditional retention"

## Resignation Timeline Template

```
D-60 (2 months before)
├── Secure the destination offer (内定)
├── Confirm the 就業規則 notice period
└── Tentatively align the start date (with the new company)

D-45 (~1.5 months before)
├── Report resignation to your direct manager (Boss Meeting)
├── Agree on the resignation date
└── Formally notify HR

D-30 (1 month before)
├── Submit the 退職届 (resignation letter)
├── Make a handover plan
├── Cooperate on selecting a successor
└── Confirm the start date with the new company

D-14 (2 weeks before)
├── Begin handover in earnest
├── Complete the handover document
├── Introduce the successor to clients/stakeholders
└── Finalize the paid-leave usage plan

D-7 (1 week before)
├── Final handover check
├── Sort personal belongings
├── Prepare to return company accounts/permissions
└── Prepare the resignation greeting mail

D-Day (last working day)
├── Send the resignation greeting mail
├── Return ID card / health-insurance card / other borrowed items
├── Greet your direct manager and colleagues in person
└── "大変お世話になりました。今後の皆様のご活躍をお祈りしております。"

D+1~ (after resignation)
├── Join the new company
├── Confirm receipt of 離職票 / 源泉徴収票
└── Switch to national health insurance / national pension (if there is a gap period)
```

## Paid Leave (有給休暇) Strategy

- Remaining paid leave is a **right**, so it can be used
- But **always coordinate** with the handover schedule
- Excessive paid-leave use that harms the team breaks 円満退職
- Pattern 1: continuous paid leave after the last working day → resignation date (most common)
- Pattern 2: intermittent use during the handover period (for job-change prep)

## Exit Ceremony Norms (退職挨拶)

### Resignation greeting mail template

> 件名: 退職のご挨拶
>
> お世話になっております。○○部の○○です。
> 私事ではございますが、○月○日をもちまして退職することとなりました。
> 在職中は大変お世話になり、心より感謝申し上げます。
> 今後の皆様のご活躍とご健勝をお祈りいたします。
>
> なお、後任は○○が担当いたします。
> 今後ともどうぞよろしくお願いいたします。

### Send timing
- Afternoon of the last working day (1–2 hours before leaving)
- Send a company-wide mail only with your manager's permission

### Gift
- Not an obligation, but it's common to leave 菓子折り (a box of sweets) for the department
- Individually wrapped is convenient (so each person can take one)
